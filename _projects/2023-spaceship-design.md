This analysis using the concepts of heat transfer is very helpful to improve my knowledge and skills as an engineer. From this study I learned more about:
1. The advantage of using heat sinks/an array of fins to dissipate heat. 
2. Cooling a resistor/wire using convection. 
<br>
<br>
The study of a heat sink dissipating heat from a source of constant heat flux demostrates the advantage of using an array of fins to decrease the temperature of the heat source. This study is parallel to cooling a cpu with a cooler. The conclusion of my analysis was that the array of 25 fins was 93.1% efficient compared to the max possible heat dissipation of the same fin structure. When using the heat sink, the cpu (modeled as a constant heat flux source) ran 89 degrees celcius cooler than not having the fin array at steady state operation. With the cpu operating at a cooler temperature its performance would be improved. Rather than upgrading a cpu for a more expensive model, one could simply improve the cooling of their cpu and improve its performance with a significantly smaller budget. 
<br>
<br>
The study of a resistor/wire submerged in oil demonstrates the time it takes for a wire to reach steady state operation given properties of the oil and the wire. The results of this study was that the time it takes for the wire to reach steady state is completely independent of the length of the wire, the time it takes to reach a certain percentage of steady state is soley dependent on the convective heat transfer coefficient, density of the wire, heat capacity, and the diameter of the wire. Any wire with the same values for these variables will all reach a specified percentage of steady state operation at the same time. If one wanted to lower the temperature of a wire/resistor could: lower the temperature of the coolant, increase the convective heat transfer coefficient, or increase the diameter of the wire. However, the diameter of the wire must not exceed 0.32 m otherwise the results of this analysis would no longer apply since the "lumped capacitance" model (ignoring the temperature gradient within the wire) was used for the wire.
